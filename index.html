HM-220 Microhardness Tester

Knoop hardness protocol for the resin composite curing study. Top and bottom surfaces, bottom-to-top hardness ratio, degree of conversion correlation. Updated after the live pilot session.

Locked Parameters
The Full Force Cycle

Each specimen is tested on the top surface (light-facing) and the bottom surface, 5 Knoop indents per surface. The bottom-to-top hardness ratio is the proxy for depth of cure and gets correlated with degree of conversion from the FTIR. Every parameter below is locked for the entire study; nothing changes between specimens, surfaces, or materials.

Non-Negotiables
Test 24 hours after light curing. Store specimens dry, in the dark, at 37 °C until testing. Never change force, timing, or objective mid-study. Handle specimens with gloves only, by the edges. If anything looks off, stop and flag it; do not improvise.
Why Knoop and why these numbers: Knoop is the indenter for polymers and composites because its geometry accounts for the elastic rebound these materials show (Vickers is for brittle materials like ceramics). At 50 gf the indent goes only about 5 to 6 µm deep and samples the surface layer, which matches what the FTIR sees. Lower force inflates hardness and scatter; higher force digs into subsurface filler and reads artificially high. Fine for mechanical property studies, wrong for a curing correlation.
Batch Workflow
How the Study Runs
Item Plan
Throughput About 10 minutes per specimen (roughly 3 to 5 minutes per surface including flip and re-zero). Batches of ~25 specimens; a weekend session can clear about 50.
Tracking Keep a checklist: specimen ID, top done, bottom done, values logged, flags. Check off each specimen only after the values are confirmed on the computer.
File naming Results: material_shade_specimen_surface, for example FS_A2_S03_TOP, VIT_A2_S07_BOT. Save to Documents / test results.
Pattern One saved 5-point pattern (dice-face layout: four corners plus center) reused for every surface of every specimen. Load it; never re-click points by hand.
Top vs bottom Identify surfaces from the specimen markings, never from the hardness values. The bottom is usually softer, but that is a result, not an identifier.
Once Per Session
Power On and Verify
1
Power On, In Order
Computer first (no sign-in needed), then the control box, then the HM-220 itself, then launch the AVPAK software (shortcut is on the desktop). No restart ritual needed; a normal boot is fine.
2
Confirm the Stage Is Down
The stage must have been left at its lowest position by the previous user. If it is up, lower it before anything else so the lens turret never collides with a specimen.
3
Calibration Check
Run one indent on the certified standard block at 0.05 kgf and log the reading in the study log. The long straight lines on the block are position references; the small marks are previous indents. If the reading is off from the certificate value, stop and report before testing specimens.
4
Verify Test Conditions
Open Set Up Test Conditions and confirm every value in the table below. Do this every session, even if you were the last user.

Controls Reference
Left Click + Drag
Moves the stage in X/Y. Direction is counterintuitive; you get used to it. The control box joystick does the same and also drives Z.
Right Click + Drag
Focus (Z) and light intensity micro-adjustments.
Optimize Light
Auto-sets illumination. Slightly reduced light often shows indent tips better on these translucent composites.
Lens Buttons
10X to locate and focus, 50X to measure. The machine switches lenses itself during a run.
The machine reference line at the edge of the image is used by the software as a surface reference. The sharper it is in focus, the better your starting point.
Repeat Per Surface
The Loop: Every Surface, Every Specimen, Same Way
The One Rule That Prevents Chaos
Start every surface with File → New Test Results then File → New Part Program (do not save when prompted), then load the saved 5-point pattern. Skipping this is what causes the point doubling (5 becomes 10 becomes 15) and the bogus Time Left values. Never re-click points by hand.
1
Clear Everything
File → New Test Results then File → New Part Program. Confirm Time Left reads zero and the pattern panel is empty.
2
Mount the Specimen
Gloves on. Check the back of the specimen for extruded composite flash before mounting; a bump on the back tilts the whole test face (this was the cause of the slanted pilot indents). Rest it flat near the center of the stage, clean interface, nothing underneath.
3
Find and Focus at 10X
Raise the stage until the surface appears, focus at 10X (easier and wider field), and pick a region away from scratches, voids, and the specimen edge. Small defects are fine as long as no indent lands on one.
4
Flatness Check (10 Seconds)
Left-click-drag across the test region. If focus holds across the area, the region is flat enough. If focus drifts noticeably, remount or pick another region; do not test on a slope.
5
Zero X and Y
Click Zero for X and Y. Z will show a small residual (for example 0.111); that is normal and fine. Zeroing lets you return to the first point later. After any flip or remount, the old zero is meaningless; always re-zero.
6
Load the Saved Pattern
In the Pattern Panel, load the study's saved 5-point pattern. If prompted to override the objective lens in the part program, click Yes so the pattern matches the current lens setting. Confirm exactly 5 points appear.
7
Run
Click Run. The machine autofocuses each point, switches to the indenter, applies the full force cycle, and moves on. About 60 to 70 seconds per point of machine time; you only work at the end, during measurement.
8
Measure, Save, Log
Verify each indent per Measure & QC, save with the study naming convention, and log the 5 individual values plus the mean in the spreadsheet before dismounting.
9
Flip and Repeat
Flip for the bottom surface and repeat from step 1. Yes, all of step 1; the clear-and-load sequence is per surface, not per specimen.
Time Left Is Not Trustworthy
The estimate is frequently wrong (8 minutes for 5 points, or over a minute for a single point). Use it only as a rough signal. What matters is the point count in the pattern panel: exactly 5, no leftovers from a previous run.
Measurement
Reading the Indents
1
Align the Video Lines
Place the two bars where the long diagonal starts and ends, at the sharp tips where the indent thins to nothing, and click to calculate. The hardness value appears immediately; no manual formula.
2
First Indent Sets the Rest
After you adjust the first indent, the software places the following lines almost exactly right, since it is the same material with little variability. Still glance at every one before confirming.
3
Manage the Light
If tips are washed out, reduce the light slightly; too much light hides the thin ends of the diamond and makes you underestimate the diagonal.
4
Inspect Any Suspect Point
Click a result and use Move to Test Position to drive the stage back to that indent and recenter it for a closer look.
Lens choice: 50X is the default for measurement precision. If indent wings keep running past the 50X field, measuring at 10X is acceptable for this study and shows the whole indent; if you switch, switch for everything and update the part program lens (say Yes to the override prompt). Do not mix lenses within the study.
Valid Indent Checklist
Symmetry: the two halves of the long diagonal within about 10 percent of each other; a stretched wing means tilt.
Focus: both tips sharp at the same time.
Depth: roughly 4.5 to 6.5 µm at this force. A point near 8 µm or more hit a slope or defect.
Placement: not on a scratch, void, or groove; fully inside the field of view.
Indents that fail are discarded and replaced with a new point nearby, never averaged in.

Sanity Band
Literature for this material class runs roughly 50 to 80 KHN for well cured surfaces. Depth of penetration does not track hardness one-to-one, so do not judge a point by its depth alone; judge by the checklist. Low bottom values are data, not mistakes; record them as measured. A whole surface far outside the band, or wild scatter across the 5 points, gets flagged before moving on.
Known Quirks
When AVPAK Fights Back
Symptom Cause and Fix
Point count doubles (5, 10, 15) across samples The part program accumulates points. Fix: New Test Results + New Part Program before every surface, then load the saved pattern. Never build on a previous run.
Time Left shows a nonsense value Leftover part program state. Clear both (results and part program) and reload the pattern. Then ignore Time Left and trust the point count.
Applied pattern does not appear on screen Known display quirk, often after applying with a different zero. Clear both, re-zero, reload the pattern.
Cannot re-run after a test or a flip A tested point stays consumed; the software will not run it again. New Test Results + New Part Program, re-zero on the new surface, reload the pattern.
Prompt: override objective lens in part program? The pattern was saved under a different lens than the current test conditions. Click Yes so they match. Lens in the part program and in test conditions must always agree.
Prompt about test force mismatch at Run Click No to keep the part program settings, then verify force reads 0.05 kgf before continuing.
Z will not zero Normal. Zero X and Y; a small Z residual is fine.
Stretched, asymmetric indents on a surface that looks in focus The specimen is tilted by its underside, usually extruded composite flash on the back, not by the test face. Inspect and clean the back and the stage; flag specimens with heavy back flash for correction at fabrication. Focus looking flat does not guarantee parallel; the indent symmetry is the real test.
One point much deeper than the rest (8+ µm) Slope, groove, or defect under that point. Discard, add a replacement point elsewhere.
Indent wing runs past the field of view at 50X Usually a tilted, stretched indent; discard it. If valid indents also overrun, measure at 10X consistently (see Measure & QC).
Hardness reads high after a force experiment Higher force reaches subsurface filler and inflates values. The study force is 0.05 kgf, always. If test conditions were touched for any reason, restore them and re-verify the full table before testing specimens.
Autofocus or edge detection hunting Translucent, glassy surface. Reduce the light slightly, refocus manually with right-click drag, and confirm the machine reference line is sharp.
Specimen Fabrication Note
The pilot tilt traced back to specimen backs, not the machine: excess composite extruded on the underside during fabrication rocks the disk. When making specimens, press the glass slides firmly and evenly, and check each specimen's back for flash before it enters the test queue. Fixing this at fabrication beats fixing it at the tester.
End of Session
Shutdown Sequence
1
Save and Log
All results saved with study names; all values in the spreadsheet; checklist updated; specimens back in dry, dark storage.
2
Lower the Stage Completely
Mandatory every time the machine is turned off. This protects the lens turret and indenters at the next startup.
3
Power Down, In Order
Close the software, turn off the machine, turn off the control box, shut down the computer through the operating system. Turning the computer fully off at the end of the day means the next session starts fresh with no restart needed.
Order
Save → Stage Down → Software → Machine → Control Box → Computer
